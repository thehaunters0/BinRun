BINRUN FIELD GUIDE

How to run your business

The whole process, in order

1
Customers

Add each household once. Set its address, garbage day, service package, price, and payment timing.

2
Schedule

BinRun turns each active subscription into service jobs. Each job is one household’s work for one collection week.

3
Route

Choose the collection week, build the take-out route the evening before, then perform the return checks on collection day.

4
Ops

Keep a record of what happened, log exceptions, follow up with customers, and adjust for holiday collection changes.

5
Payments

Check invoices and record actual Cash or Apple Cash received. Outstanding balances update automatically.

6
Growth & Reports

Track people who may become customers, marketing efforts, expenses, and business performance.

You do not have to use every section every day. Most days you will use Schedule, Route, and Payments. Ops is for records and problems; Growth is for finding customers.

Customers — your permanent household list

  1. Tap Add and enter the customer's name, full address, and contact details.
  2. Set the municipal garbage collection weekday and the first collection date. The first collection date must be the actual date the truck is expected.
  3. Choose a service plan. Full service is both bins out and back. Your standard prices are $5 for one week, $8 for two, $12 for three, and $15 for four. Partial service is generally $3 per week; custom combinations should use the agreed total.
  4. Check the task boxes carefully. They define what BinRun should do for every week (or each custom week).
  5. Choose when payment is due, enter the agreed price, and add placement/access notes. Record terms acceptance only after the customer actually agrees.
  6. Save. Do not add them again next week—the subscription creates future jobs for the same customer.

Paused customers stop generating future unfinished jobs. Cancelled customers are no longer active. Edit them if they restart.

Schedule — the calendar of work to do

A customer is the household. A job is one service week for that household. One customer on a 4-week package normally has four separate scheduled jobs.

  • The date shown is collection day (when the city truck is expected).
  • Take-out is planned for 7:00 PM the day before.
  • Return checks happen on collection day at 10:00 AM, 11:30 AM, and 1:00 PM.
  • Tap Use this week in Route for the collection week you are about to service. This tells Route which weekly jobs to show.

If no work appears, check that the customer is active and has a subscription, collection day, and first collection date. Use Refresh after editing customers.

Route — where you perform the service

Night before collection: choose Take bins out, review all selected customers, optimize the order, and follow the route. Mark the required take-out checklist as complete at each customer.

Collection day: choose Bring bins back and start with Sweep 1 at 10:00 AM. Visit every customer whose bins have not been returned, even if you suspect the truck has not arrived. This first visit is how you check.

  • If the truck collected the garbage, tap Collected — return bins and mark the return complete.
  • If the truck has not collected it, tap Still not collected. The customer stays pending.
  • At Sweep 2 (11:30 AM), visit everyone still pending. Repeat at Sweep 3 (1:00 PM) only for those still pending.
  • Never assign a customer to a sweep in advance. The sweep is simply the next time you check all bins that have not yet been returned.

When no customers remain pending, you are finished. You can skip later sweeps. The route order is a suggested travel order, not an automatic service completion.

Ops — your service notebook and exceptions desk

  • Daily run sheet: a quick count of take-outs, returns, and completed jobs for the selected week.
  • Service activity: add notes, customer contacts, photos, and evidence. Route checklist changes are also logged automatically.
  • Issues: record things such as a truck not collecting, damaged/missing bins, blocked access, bad weather, or a requested change. Mark the issue resolved only after you have handled it.
  • Customer communication: prepare a reminder or update, review it, and then send it yourself through Messages or Mail. BinRun does not send it automatically.
  • Holiday adjustments: check the municipality's official notice, then move the affected service date. BinRun does not automatically know city holiday schedules.
  • Business terms: keep your standard service terms and contact details in one place.
  • Discounts/refunds: use only when changing a customer's invoice or recording money actually returned.

Payments — invoices are not the same as money received

  • An invoice is the bill: what the customer owes for a package or weekly service.
  • A payment is money you physically received. Record the date, amount, and whether it was Cash or Apple Cash.
  • Partial payments are okay. The remaining balance is calculated automatically.
  • A refund is a separate record for money you gave back. A discount changes the invoice amount owed; it is not a payment.
  • Check the customer's history for their invoices, service weeks, payments, refunds, and balance.

BinRun does not transfer money, charge cards, or confirm Apple Cash automatically. Only mark money received after you verify it in person or in Wallet.

Growth — before and around the customer relationship

  • A lead is someone who asked about BinRun but has not become a customer yet. Add them, choose a stage, and set a follow-up date.
  • When they agree to service, choose Convert to customer. This creates the permanent customer profile so you do not have to retype everything.
  • A campaign records a marketing effort such as handing out flyers, posting in a neighborhood group, or asking for referrals. Record its cost and how many people responded.
  • An expense records business spending such as printing, bags, supplies, or equipment.
  • Reports summarize your recorded activity. Keep records current for useful numbers.

You do not need to create a campaign for every conversation. Use Growth when you are actively trying to get more customers or want to understand which methods work.

Database & cloud syncing — what happens behind the scenes

Vercel is where the BinRun website lives. Supabase is the online database and owner sign-in service. Your browser also keeps a local copy so the app can open on that device.

This backend currently stores one private BinRun workspace per owner account. The workspace contains your customer list, service jobs, invoices, payments, notes, and settings together as one structured data record. It is a real hosted database, but it is not yet one separate database table for every kind of business record.

  1. In Supabase, create your Personal organization and a project named BinRun. The organization is just the account container; it is not your customer list.
  2. Run the supplied SQL once in Supabase SQL Editor. Add only the Project URL and Publishable key to backend-config.js.
  3. Deploy the contents of binrun_app to your existing Vercel project.
  4. On the device that already has your customer records, sign in to BinRun and choose Upload this device's data once to create the first cloud copy.
  5. On your iPhone, open the same website, sign in with the same owner account, and choose Load cloud data onto this device.
  6. After both devices are initialized, new changes sync while online. Export a JSON backup regularly anyway.

If BinRun detects that both devices changed independently, sync pauses. Export a backup and deliberately choose which copy to keep. Do not share your owner password with customers. The owner sign-in is not a customer portal.

Privacy: only put ordinary service details in BinRun. Never store passwords, payment-card numbers, or highly sensitive information. Never place a Supabase secret/service-role key in the website.

Your normal weekly routine

  1. Before a new customer starts: create their profile and check the weekday, first date, package, tasks, price, payment timing, and access instructions.
  2. Before service: review Schedule, confirm the city collection date, and select that week.
  3. At 7 PM the prior evening: open Route → Take bins out, build the route, complete every required take-out.
  4. On collection day: Route → Bring bins back → Sweep 1. Visit all still-pending customers and either return their bins or mark not collected.
  5. At 11:30 AM and 1 PM: repeat for remaining pending customers only. Stop early when none remain.
  6. When payment is received: open Payments and record the amount and method. Follow up on any open balance.
  7. After a problem: log it in Ops and leave it open until resolved.
  8. Once a week: review Growth leads, record expenses, glance at Reports, and export a backup.

What BinRun does not do yet

It does not automatically contact customers, receive or transfer payments, confirm municipal collection status, or provide customer login accounts. It also does not automatically import city holiday calendars. The next major expansion would be individual customer accounts and a customer portal, after the owner database setup has been proven with your real records.
